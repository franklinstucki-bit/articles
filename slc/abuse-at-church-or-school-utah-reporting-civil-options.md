Finding out that a child was hurt by someone at church or school is one of the hardest things a family can face. These are places built on trust. For many Utah families, they are also the center of community life, which can make speaking up feel even harder.

This guide, from Frank Stucki, Esq., MBA of SLC Survivor Law, is for survivors and parents. It explains how to report abuse in Utah and what civil options may exist afterward, in plain language.

**The short answer:** If a child is in danger right now, call 911. To report child abuse in Utah, call the Division of Child and Family Services (DCFS) intake hotline at 1-855-323-3237, available 24 hours a day, or contact local law enforcement. Separately, survivors and parents may have civil claims against the person who caused the harm and, in some cases, against the school or organization that failed to protect the child. Claims against public schools have a one-year notice deadline.

## How to Report Child Abuse in Utah

### Call 911 for emergencies

If a child is in immediate danger, has visible injuries, or is afraid to go home, call 911 or your local police department.

### Call the DCFS hotline

Utah's DCFS intake hotline is **1-855-323-DCFS (3237)**. It is answered 24 hours a day, 7 days a week. DCFS also has an online reporting form, but it is meant only for situations where no child is in immediate danger, and online reports take longer to process than calls.

You do not need proof to make a report. You need a reason to believe abuse happened. DCFS and law enforcement do the investigating.

### Report to law enforcement

You can also report to the police department or sheriff's office where the abuse happened. Sexual abuse and physical abuse can be crimes, and a criminal case is separate from any civil case. If you are an adult survivor reporting abuse from your childhood, law enforcement is usually the right place to start.

### Utah's universal reporting law

Utah is a universal reporting state. Under Utah Code 80-2-602, any person who has reason to believe a child has been abused or neglected must immediately report it to DCFS or to the nearest peace officer or law enforcement agency. This applies to everyone, not only teachers, doctors and counselors.

There is a narrow exception for clergy. It covers a confession made directly to a member of the clergy acting in a ministerial capacity, when the clergy member is bound by church doctrine or practice to keep it confidential. Clergy who learn about abuse from any other source must still report. We cover the clergy exception in more detail in a separate article.

## Civil Options After Abuse at a Utah School or Church

A criminal case is about punishing the person who did it. A civil case is about the survivor: accountability, answers, and money to pay for counseling, lost opportunities and the harm done. A civil case can move forward whether or not criminal charges are filed.

### Claims against the person who caused the harm

For sexual abuse of a child, Utah Code 78B-2-308 allows a survivor to sue the person who committed the abuse at any time. There is no deadline for that claim.

### Claims against a school, church or organization

An organization is not automatically responsible because one of its people did something wrong. But it can be responsible for its own failures. In general terms, these claims often involve:

- **Negligent hiring:** bringing someone into a position of trust without reasonable screening.
- **Negligent supervision:** failing to reasonably supervise staff, volunteers or the children in its care.
- **Negligent retention:** keeping someone in a position of access to children after warning signs or complaints.
- **Failure to act on reports:** ignoring or mishandling information that a child was at risk.

Under Utah Code 78B-2-308, child sexual abuse claims against someone other than the person who did it generally must be filed within four years after the survivor turns 18, or within four years after the survivor discovers the abuse if that happens later. The law also says a parent's or guardian's knowledge is not held against a child under 18.

## Public Schools in Utah: The One-Year Notice of Claim

Public schools and school districts are government entities. Claims against them fall under the Governmental Immunity Act of Utah, which has its own strict rules.

- **Notice within one year.** Under Utah Code 63G-7-402, a claim against a government entity or its employees is barred unless a written notice of claim is filed within one year after the claim arises.
- **When a claim "arises."** Under Utah Code 63G-7-401, the clock generally starts when the claimant knew, or with reasonable diligence should have known, about the claim and which government entity was involved.
- **Where it goes.** For a school district, the notice goes to the superintendent or business administrator of the school board.
- **What comes next.** A lawsuit generally cannot be filed until 60 days after the notice, and must be filed within two years after the claim arose (Utah Code 63G-7-403).

These deadlines are short and unforgiving. If a public school may be involved, talk with a lawyer early, even if you are still deciding what to do.

## Private Schools and Churches in Utah

Private schools, religious schools and churches are not government entities, so the public-school notice rules generally do not apply. Instead, claims usually follow ordinary Utah negligence principles and the time limits in 78B-2-308 for child sexual abuse claims.

Many faith communities work hard to protect children, and most leaders and volunteers would never harm a child. A civil claim is not an attack on anyone's faith. It asks a specific question: did this organization take reasonable steps to keep this child safe, and if not, who is responsible for the harm?

Survivors from faith communities often worry about their family, their ward or congregation, and their own beliefs. Those feelings are valid. You can take your time, and you can ask questions confidentially before deciding anything.

## Title IX in Utah Schools

Title IX is a federal law (20 U.S.C. 1681) that prohibits sex discrimination in education programs that receive federal funding. Sexual harassment and sexual abuse can fall under Title IX, and schools covered by it have obligations to respond.

Title IX applies to public schools and to many private schools that receive federal funds. It includes an exemption for schools controlled by religious organizations when following a specific requirement would conflict with the organization's religious tenets. A Title IX complaint is not the same as a civil lawsuit, and it does not replace one.

## FAQ

### How do I report child abuse in Utah?

Call Utah's DCFS intake hotline at 1-855-323-DCFS (3237), which is available 24 hours a day, or contact local law enforcement. If a child is in immediate danger, call 911. DCFS also has an online form, but only for situations where no child is in immediate danger.

### Does everyone in Utah have to report child abuse?

Yes. Under Utah Code 80-2-602, any person who has reason to believe a child has been abused or neglected must immediately report it to DCFS or to law enforcement. There is a narrow exception for certain confessions made to clergy, and clergy must still report if they learn of abuse another way.

### Can I sue a church in Utah for abuse that happened when I was a child?

Possibly. A church or other organization can be held responsible for its own negligence, such as failing to supervise or keeping someone in a role with children after warning signs. Under Utah Code 78B-2-308, those claims generally must be filed within four years after turning 18 or within four years after discovering the abuse, whichever is later.

### How long do I have to file a claim against a Utah public school?

A written notice of claim must generally be filed within one year after the claim arises, under Utah Code 63G-7-402. For a school district, the notice goes to the superintendent or business administrator. Missing this deadline can end the claim, so talk with a lawyer early.

### Is there a time limit to sue the person who abused me as a child in Utah?

No. Utah Code 78B-2-308 allows a survivor of child sexual abuse to sue the person who committed the abuse at any time. Claims against organizations have separate deadlines.

### Is a Title IX complaint the same as a lawsuit?

No. Title IX is a federal law that requires federally funded schools to respond to sex discrimination, including sexual harassment and abuse. A Title IX complaint goes through the school or federal agency process, while a civil lawsuit is filed in court and can seek compensation.
