You bought a pressure cooker to make dinner easier. You did not expect it to throw boiling food at you. If a Power Quick Pot burned you or someone you love, you are probably hurting, frustrated and unsure what to do next. This guide covers what federal safety officials announced, what to do right now, and how to protect your rights.

## What the CPSC announced

On October 15, 2026, the U.S. Consumer Product Safety Commission (CPSC) issued [Product Safety Warning No. 27-030](https://www.cpsc.gov/Warnings/2027/CPSC-Warns-Consumers-to-Immediately-Stop-Using-Tristar-Products-6-Quart-Power-Quick-Pot-Pressure-Cookers-Due-to-Risk-of-Serious-Injury-from-Burn-Hazard). It urges consumers to stop using **Power Quick Pot 6-Quart Pressure Cookers, model Y6D-36**, immediately.

- **Importer:** Tristar Products, Inc., of Altamonte Springs, Florida. The cookers were made in China.
- **Units:** About 393,000.
- **Where they were sold:** Walmart.com, Amazon.com, eBay.com and other retailers nationwide.
- **When:** About July 2018 through November 2023.
- **Price:** $44 to $160.
- **How to identify it:** A stainless-steel cooker with a black pressure lid, button controls and a digital display. "Power Quick Pot" is printed on the front, and the model number is on a label on the back.

**There is no recall.** According to the CPSC, Tristar has refused to recall the cookers or offer consumers a remedy. That means no refund, no repair and no replacement. It also means many owners may never hear about the danger unless someone tells them.

## The hazard and reported injuries

The CPSC says it has received 24 reports of these cookers expelling hot contents unexpectedly, causing serious burn injuries.

Burns from pressurized steam and boiling food can be deep. They often hit the face, chest, arms and hands, because that is where people are when they stand near a cooker. Children in the kitchen are at particular risk.

## What to do right now

1. **Stop using it.** Unplug the cooker and put it away where no one will use it.
2. **Get medical care.** If anyone was burned, see a doctor even if the burn seems minor. Steam burns can be worse than they first look, and medical records document what happened.
3. **Keep the product and the packaging.** If no one was hurt, follow the CPSC's advice to dispose of it, and do not sell it or give it away. **If someone was hurt, do not throw it out yet.** The cooker, lid, gasket, box, manual and receipt can be key evidence. Store them safely and talk to a lawyer before you dispose of anything.
4. **Don't send it back to the manufacturer if someone was injured.** Once the product leaves your hands, you may lose the chance to have it independently examined.
5. **Take photos.** Photograph the cooker, the model label on the back, the lid and locking parts, the spill area, and the injuries. Keep photographing the injuries as they heal.
6. **Write it down.** Note the date, what you were cooking, how full the pot was, and exactly what happened.
7. **Report it.** You can report the incident at SaferProducts.gov or call the CPSC Hotline at 800-638-2772.

## When a burn injury may be a claim

A pressure cooker should keep its hot contents sealed in while it is under pressure. When one expels scalding food and burns someone, product liability law may let the injured person hold responsible the companies in the chain of distribution. Depending on the state, that can include the manufacturer, the importer and sometimes the sellers. A CPSC warning does not prove a case on its own, but it is important information about a known hazard.

A claim may be worth looking into if:

- You or a family member needed medical treatment for a burn from a Power Quick Pot.
- The burn left scarring, needed follow-up care, or caused missed work.
- The cooker was being used as directed.
