**Quick answer:** Washington requires motorcycle operators and passengers to wear a DOT-compliant helmet with the strap fastened, under RCW 46.37.530. If you were hurt without one, the other side may raise it, but it does not by itself erase a claim against a driver who caused the crash.

You know the feeling. SR-20 through the North Cascades on a dry August morning. Chuckanut Drive with the bay below. Even a gray commute on I-5 feels different on two wheels.

Then a driver turns left across your lane. Afterward, someone asks about your helmet.

## What does Washington's helmet law require?

[RCW 46.37.530](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=46.37.530) requires anyone who operates or rides on a motorcycle, motor-driven cycle or moped on a state highway, county road or city street to wear a motorcycle helmet, with the neck or chin strap securely fastened while moving.

The statute defines a motorcycle helmet as one with a hard outer shell, padding, and a strap retention system, certified by the manufacturer as meeting the federal standard at 49 C.F.R. 571.218. In everyday terms, look for DOT certification.

The law also:

- Requires eye protection (glasses, goggles or a face shield) when the motorcycle has no windshield
- Prohibits carrying a child under five on a motorcycle or motor-driven cycle
- Makes it unlawful to sell a helmet that does not meet the requirements
- Has narrow exceptions, such as certain enclosed vehicles with seat belts and antique motor-driven cycles

## Does not wearing a helmet hurt my claim?

It can complicate it. Insurers may argue that a missing or non-compliant helmet made a head injury worse, and that damages should be reduced for that part of the harm.

Whether helmet use comes up in a claim, and how, depends on the facts and the law that applies to them. It is a question worth reviewing with a lawyer early.

What stays true: helmet use speaks to injuries, not to who caused the crash. A driver who turned left in front of you is still the driver who turned left in front of you.

## How does comparative fault fit in?

Under [RCW 4.22.005](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=4.22.005), any fault assigned to an injured person reduces the award proportionately but does not bar recovery. Arguments about helmets, lane position and speed tend to be fought inside that framework.

## What if I was wearing a helmet?

Keep it. Do not throw it away, repair it or sell it. A damaged helmet can show where and how hard your head struck. It also helps answer a defense argument before it starts.

Keep your gear too: jacket, gloves, boots. Photograph everything before it is cleaned.

## What coverage applies to riders?

Riders face coverage gaps car drivers often do not:

- **PIP:** The [Insurance Commissioner](https://www.insurance.wa.gov/insurance-resources/auto-insurance/how-auto-insurance-works/personal-injury-protection-pip) lists motorcycles among standard PIP exclusions, though motorcycle coverage may be available.
- **UIM:** Under [RCW 48.22.030(10)](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=48.22.030), insurers must give named insureds on motorcycle policies the opportunity to reject underinsured coverage in writing. Check whether you kept it.
- **Liability coverage of the at-fault driver:** Often the main source, and sometimes limited.

## Local specifics

Washington riding brings its own risks. Wet leaves and painted lines in Seattle and Tacoma. Gravel on the shoulders of island roads in the San Juans. Grooved pavement and expansion joints on I-90 and SR-520 bridges. Deer near Bellingham and Spokane at dusk. Riders are harder for drivers to see in rain and low light, which is often the core of the case.

## FAQ

### Is a novelty helmet legal in Washington?

The statute requires a helmet certified by the manufacturer as meeting the federal standard. A helmet without that certification may not comply.

### Do passengers have to wear helmets?

Yes. RCW 46.37.530 applies to anyone who operates or rides on a motorcycle on the covered roads.

### Should I talk to the insurer about my helmet?

Talk to a lawyer before giving the other driver's insurer a recorded statement; your own insurer may require cooperation under your policy.
