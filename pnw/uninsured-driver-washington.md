**Quick answer:** If the driver who hit you had no insurance, too little insurance, or drove away, your own underinsured motorist (UIM) coverage may step in. Washington insurers must include this coverage unless it was rejected in writing, and phantom-vehicle claims carry a 72-hour police-report requirement.

The other driver is apologetic. Then the other driver says the words nobody wants to hear: "I don't have insurance."

Or there is no other driver at all. Just taillights disappearing down Aurora Avenue, or a pickup that crowded you off SR-20 and kept going.

## Isn't insurance required in Washington?

Yes. [RCW 46.30.020](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=46.30.020) requires people driving a registered vehicle in Washington to have liability insurance or another approved form of financial responsibility, with some exceptions. The minimum liability limits in [RCW 46.29.090](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=46.29.090) are $25,000 per person and $50,000 per accident for bodily injury, and $10,000 for property damage.

Required is not the same as present. Some drivers carry nothing. Many carry only the minimum, which serious injuries can quickly exceed.

## What is UIM coverage?

In Washington, "underinsured" coverage does double duty. Under [RCW 48.22.030](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=48.22.030), it protects insureds who are legally entitled to recover from underinsured, hit-and-run and phantom vehicles. The statute's definition of an underinsured vehicle includes one with no applicable liability coverage at all.

Insurers must include it in Washington policies unless a named insured or spouse rejects it in writing, subject to exceptions in the statute.

## What if I was on a motorcycle?

The statute treats motorcycles differently. Motorcycles are excepted from the general requirement, but RCW 48.22.030(10) requires insurers to give named insureds the opportunity to reject the coverage in writing. Riders should pull their motorcycle policy and look for UIM on the declarations page.

## What is a phantom vehicle?

A phantom vehicle causes a crash without touching you. A car swerves into your lane on I-5, you take the shoulder to avoid it, and you hit the barrier.

Under RCW 48.22.030(8), a phantom-vehicle claim requires that the crash be reported to law enforcement within seventy-two hours. The facts must also be corroborated by competent evidence other than the testimony of the insured or anyone else making a UIM claim from the same crash.

That corroboration can be an independent witness, video, or physical evidence. Dash cams and doorbell cameras have become important here.

## What should I do after a hit-and-run or uninsured crash?

The [Insurance Commissioner's guidance](https://www.insurance.wa.gov/insurance-resources/auto-insurance/auto-insurance-claims/what-do-if-youre-hit-uninsured-or-underinsured-driver) lines up with what we tell clients:

1. Call 911 if anyone is hurt. Stay at the scene.
2. Call the police, and report a phantom-vehicle crash within 72 hours.
3. Photograph the scene, the damage, and the other driver and vehicle if present.
4. Look for cameras: businesses, homes, transit, traffic.
5. Notify your own insurer and ask about UIM, PIP and collision claims.

A driver who leaves an injury crash has broken the law under [RCW 46.52.020](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=46.52.020). That is a criminal matter. Your injury claim runs on a separate track.

## Is my own insurer on my side in a UIM claim?

It is your insurer, and it owes duties to you. In a UIM claim, though, it also steps into the shoes of the at-fault driver on questions of fault and damages. Expect it to evaluate the claim carefully.

Treat a UIM claim with the same care you would bring to a claim against a stranger's insurer.

## Local specifics

Hit-and-runs in Seattle, Tacoma and Spokane often happen at night, in rain, with low visibility. Island and rural crashes, on Orcas, Lopez or the Chuckanut Drive curves, may have no witnesses at all. In those places, physical evidence and an early police report carry even more weight.

## FAQ

### Will a UIM claim raise my premium?

That depends on your insurer and the circumstances. Ask your carrier directly.

### Can I use UIM if the other driver had minimum limits?

Possibly. UIM can apply when the at-fault driver's coverage is not enough to cover your damages, depending on your policy.

### What if I never got a police report?

For a phantom-vehicle claim, the 72-hour report rule matters. Talk with a lawyer quickly about what you have.
