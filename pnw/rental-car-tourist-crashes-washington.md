**Quick answer:** A crash involving a rental car or an out-of-state driver in Washington is handled under Washington law, but the insurance can come from several places: the driver's own policy, the rental company, a credit card, or your own coverage. Gathering the right documents before anyone leaves town makes the claim much easier to sort out.

They land at Sea-Tac, pick up the keys, and head north on I-5 toward the Anacortes ferry or east toward Snoqualmie. The roads are new to them. So are the merges, the rain and the ferry lines.

Visitors make the Northwest what it is. They also make crash claims more complicated.

## Which law applies if the driver is from out of state?

A crash on a Washington road is generally governed by Washington's rules of the road and Washington's fault rules. That includes [pure comparative fault under RCW 4.22.005](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=4.22.005) and the [three-year filing deadline in RCW 4.16.080](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=4.16.080) for most injury lawsuits.

Under [RCW 46.30.020(1)(b)](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=46.30.020), a driver of a vehicle registered in another state that requires insurance must provide that state's proof of insurance when an officer asks.

## Where does the insurance come from in a rental car crash?

Possible layers include:

- The rental driver's own auto policy, which may extend to rentals
- Coverage purchased at the rental counter
- Coverage the rental company carries on its fleet
- Benefits tied to the credit card used for the rental, usually for vehicle damage
- Your own coverage, including [PIP](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=48.22.085) and [UIM](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=48.22.030), if the other driver's coverage falls short

Whether the rental company itself has responsibility depends on the facts and on state and federal law. The driver's conduct and coverage usually come first.

## What if I was the visitor who got hurt?

You may be flying home in two days. Use them well:

1. Get medical care here and keep copies of everything.
2. Get the police report number before you leave.
3. Photograph the other driver's license, insurance card and plate, with their permission.
4. Photograph the rental agreement if you were driving a rental.
5. Notify your own insurer and the rental company in writing.
6. Write down what happened while it is fresh, including the road and direction of travel.

Washington's deadlines keep running after you go home. Many steps in a Washington claim can be handled remotely.

## What if a tourist hit me?

Get everything at the scene. Out-of-state and international drivers can be hard to reach later. If the driver is in a rental, also note the rental company and the agreement number if they will share it.

If the driver leaves the scene, it becomes a hit-and-run. Report it promptly and see our article on uninsured and phantom-vehicle claims.

## Do I need to file a crash report?

If no officer makes a report and the crash caused injury, death or damage above the State Patrol's threshold, [RCW 46.52.030](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=46.52.030) requires a written report within four days. For visitors, it is easiest to handle before leaving the state.

## Local specifics

Tourist crashes cluster where visitors cluster: the Sea-Tac rental return loop, downtown Seattle near Pike Place, I-5 near the Canadian border at Blaine, ferry lines in Anacortes, Edmonds and Seattle's Colman Dock, the roads around Friday Harbor and Orcas, and the passes on the way to Leavenworth or Spokane. Drivers from British Columbia are common on I-5 and around Bellingham. Their insurance may come from a Canadian carrier, which can add steps.

## FAQ

### Will my credit card cover my injuries?

Card benefits more often address damage to the rental car than bodily injury. Read the card's benefit terms.

### Should I buy the rental counter coverage?

That depends on your own policy and card benefits. Check before you travel.

### Can I pursue a Washington claim from another state?

Yes. Washington claims can be pursued while you live elsewhere. Start early.
