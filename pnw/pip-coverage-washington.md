**Quick answer:** Personal injury protection, or PIP, is coverage on your own auto policy that can help pay medical bills, lost wages and certain other costs after a crash, generally regardless of who caused it. Washington insurers must offer it, and you keep it unless you rejected it in writing.

The first bill usually arrives before the bruising fades. An ER visit in Tacoma. Imaging in Everett. A follow-up with a doctor in Bellevue who wants you back in two weeks.

The other driver's insurer is still "investigating." PIP is often what keeps care moving while the fault questions get sorted out.

## What is PIP in Washington?

Under [RCW 48.22.085](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=48.22.085), an insurer cannot issue or renew an auto liability policy in Washington unless PIP is offered. The named insured may reject it, but the rejection has to be in writing.

The [Washington Insurance Commissioner's PIP page](https://www.insurance.wa.gov/insurance-resources/auto-insurance/how-auto-insurance-works/personal-injury-protection-pip) explains that PIP applies no matter who is at fault, with some exclusions.

## What does PIP pay for?

[RCW 48.22.095](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=48.22.095) sets the minimum benefits an insurer must offer for each insured person:

- **Medical and hospital:** $10,000
- **Funeral:** $2,000
- **Income continuation:** $10,000, limited to $200 per week
- **Loss of services:** $5,000, limited to $200 per week

The Commissioner's page notes that higher limits are available, and that medical benefits are available for three years after the accident. It also describes a waiting period before income benefits begin. Your own policy controls the details, so the declarations page is the first thing to pull.

## Who does PIP cover?

According to the Commissioner, PIP generally covers the named policyholder, related household members, and non-family passengers and pedestrians involved in an accident with the insured vehicle.

That matters for pedestrians in Seattle's busy corridors and for passengers in a friend's car. You may have PIP available through more than one policy.

## Does PIP cover motorcycle crashes?

Usually not by default. The Commissioner lists motorcycles among the standard PIP exclusions, noting motorcycle coverage may be available but is usually expensive. Riders should check their own motorcycle policy for any medical-payments coverage.

If you were riding and were hit by a car, other coverage may still apply. That depends on the policies involved.

## Do I have to pay PIP back?

Sometimes, in part. When a claim later resolves against the at-fault driver, your PIP insurer may seek reimbursement from that recovery. How that works, and what share of fees and costs the PIP carrier bears, depends on the facts and the policy.

This is one reason to track every PIP payment from day one.

## Practical steps for using PIP

1. Report the crash to your own insurer promptly, even if you were not at fault.
2. Ask for the PIP claim number and the adjuster's direct contact.
3. Give your medical providers that claim number so bills go to PIP first.
4. Keep a simple log of missed work days, mileage to appointments, and household help you had to hire.
5. Read any PIP application or authorization before signing. Ask what it allows.

A PIP adjuster is your own insurer. That relationship still has rules, and Washington law expects insurers to handle claims fairly.

## FAQ

### Is PIP required in Washington?

Insurers must offer it. You have it unless the named insured rejected it in writing.

### Will using PIP raise my rates?

That depends on your insurer and the circumstances. Ask your carrier directly.

### Can I use my health insurance instead?

You may be able to use both. Coordinating them is often worth a conversation before bills go to collections.
