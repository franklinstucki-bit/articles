**Quick answer:** Crashes on Snoqualmie Pass (I-90) and Stevens Pass (US 2) often involve snow, black ice, spinouts and chain-up zones. Fault usually turns on whether each driver slowed for conditions, kept a safe distance, and followed WSDOT's posted traction and chain requirements.

The climb out of North Bend starts in rain. Somewhere past the summit at Snoqualmie, the rain turns to slush, then to something you cannot see at all.

On US 2, the road narrows past Skykomish. Trucks grind upward. Skiers heading home from Stevens Pass come down fast, tired, with the light gone.

## Why do pass crashes happen?

Mountain passes compress every winter hazard into a few miles:

- Black ice on shaded curves and bridge decks
- Sudden whiteouts and snow squalls
- Spinouts that block lanes around a blind bend
- Jackknifed trucks and vehicles stalled at chain-up areas
- Drivers who do not have the traction equipment the signs require

[WSDOT's winter driving guide](https://www.wsdot.wa.gov/publications/fulltext/winter/winter-driving-guide.pdf) notes that many pass closures come from vehicles without proper equipment sliding out.

## What are Washington's traction and chain rules?

WSDOT posts traction and chain requirements on the passes. According to its winter driving guide:

- "Traction Tires Advised" means oversize loads are prohibited.
- "Tire Chains Required" means vehicles need approved chains, except four- and all-wheel-drive vehicles, which must still carry them.
- "Chains Required on All Vehicles" applies in extreme weather, including four- and all-wheel-drive.
- Vehicles over 10,000 pounds gross vehicle weight rating must carry chains, under WSDOT's [chain requirements for heavier vehicles](https://wsdot.wa.gov/travel/commercial-vehicles/route-commercial-vehicle-restrictions/chain-requirements-vehicles-over-10000-pounds).

Check [WSDOT pass reports](https://wsdot.wa.gov/traffic/passes/text.aspx) before you go. Requirements change through the day.

## How is fault decided in a snow or ice crash?

Weather does not excuse careless driving. [RCW 46.61.400](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=46.61.400) requires a speed that is reasonable and prudent under the conditions, and calls for reduced speed on curves, narrow or winding roads, and when weather or highway conditions create special hazards.

In a pass crash, insurers and juries tend to ask:

- Was the driver going too fast for the visible conditions?
- Did the driver have the required traction tires or chains?
- Was there a safe following distance on a descending grade?
- Did a truck driver follow chain and speed restrictions?

Washington's [comparative fault rule](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=4.22.005) lets fault be split when more than one driver contributed.

## What if the road itself was the problem?

Sometimes questions come up about plowing, sanding, signage or closures. Claims involving the state have their own pre-suit procedure under [RCW 4.92.110](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=4.92.110). These claims are fact-heavy and timing matters, so they deserve an early look.

## What should I do after a crash on a pass?

1. Get out of the travel lanes if you can do it safely. Passes are dangerous places to stand.
2. Call 911. State Patrol often handles pass crashes.
3. Photograph the road surface, signs, and the other vehicles' tires and chains if it is safe.
4. Note the time, temperature and the pass report status if you can.
5. Get medical care. Pass crashes often mean long delays before treatment.

## Local specifics

Snoqualmie connects Seattle and Bellevue to Ellensburg, Yakima and Spokane. Stevens links Everett and Monroe to Leavenworth and Wenatchee. Visitors from out of state, skiers, and freight all share the same lanes. That mix, in the dark of a December evening, is where many pass crashes start.

Treatment may happen far from home, in Cle Elum, Ellensburg or Wenatchee. Keep records from every provider.

## FAQ

### If I had chains and the other driver didn't, does that decide fault?

It is important evidence, not an automatic answer. Speed, distance and the specific crash still matter.

### What if a spinout ahead caused a pileup?

Multi-vehicle crashes can involve several at-fault drivers. Each driver's conduct is looked at separately.

### What if I lost control on ice with no one else involved?

Your own PIP or collision coverage may help. Whether anyone else is responsible depends on the facts.
