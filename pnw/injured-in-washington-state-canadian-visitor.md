You drove down for a weekend in Seattle, a day of shopping in Bellingham, or a ski trip to Mt. Baker. Then a crash, a fall or a collision at the border changed the plan. Now you are home in BC or Alberta, sore, and unsure whether anything can be done from the other side of the line.

This guide walks you through the next steps, in plain English.

**The short answer:** If you were hurt in Washington State, you can usually bring an injury claim in Washington even though you live in Canada. Get a police report, get medical care, tell your own insurer, and keep your records. Washington's general deadline for personal injury lawsuits is three years, and much of the case can be handled remotely from home.

## Right After a Crash in Washington State

### Call the police and get a report

If anyone is hurt, call 911. In Washington, the responding agency might be the Washington State Patrol on I-5 or I-90, Bellingham or Seattle Police in the city, or a county sheriff in rural areas. Before you leave the scene, try to get:

- The officer's name and agency
- The report or incident number
- The other driver's name, licence, plate and insurance information
- Names and phone numbers of witnesses
- Photos of the vehicles, the road, and any injuries

### The Washington collision report requirement (RCW 46.52.030)

Washington law requires the driver of any vehicle in a collision that injures or kills someone to file a written report within four days, unless a law enforcement officer investigates and files the report instead. Reports are also required for property damage above a threshold set by the Washington State Patrol. If police came to the scene and took a report, that usually covers it. If no officer filed a report, ask a Washington lawyer or the local police agency what you need to do, and do it promptly.

### Border crossing incidents

Collisions in the lineups at Blaine, Lynden or Sumas can involve tight quarters and multiple vehicles. The same steps apply: get a report number if police respond, photograph everything, and exchange information.

## Getting Medical Care in the US

Your health comes first. Get checked at an emergency room or urgent care in Washington if you need it, even if you plan to follow up at home. Some injuries, like concussions and soft tissue damage, can feel minor at first.

### Travel medical insurance

US hospital bills can be large. Your provincial health plan may cover only part of emergency care out of the country. If you bought travel medical insurance, or have it through work or a credit card, call the number on your policy as soon as you can. Many policies want early notice. Keep copies of every bill, receipt and discharge paper.

### Follow up at home

Once you are back in BC or Alberta, see your family doctor and keep treating as recommended. Your Canadian medical records can be used in a Washington claim, so you do not need to keep returning to the US for care.

## How ICBC and Alberta Insurance May Fit In

### BC residents and ICBC Enhanced Care

Since May 1, 2021, BC auto insurance has worked under ICBC's Enhanced Care model. ICBC states that all British Columbians injured in a crash in Canada or the United States have access to Enhanced Accident Benefits, regardless of who caused the crash. Report the crash to ICBC and ask about your benefits.

Whether you can also pursue the at-fault Washington driver, and how that affects your ICBC benefits, depends on your situation and on BC law. Ask ICBC or a BC lawyer about the BC side. A Washington lawyer can tell you about the Washington side.

### Alberta residents

Contact your own auto insurer and report the crash. Alberta is moving to a new Care-First auto insurance system beginning January 1, 2027, so the rules that apply may depend on when your crash happened. Your insurer or an Alberta lawyer can explain what applies to you.

## You Don't Have to Live in the US to Bring a Claim in Washington

Washington courts hear claims about injuries that happen in Washington, no matter where the injured person lives. Being a Canadian resident, visitor or tourist does not prevent you from making a claim against an at-fault driver or property owner in Washington.

Washington also follows pure comparative fault (RCW 4.22.005). If you were partly at fault, your damages are reduced by your share, but your claim is not barred.

## Handling a Washington Injury Case From Canada

Most of a Washington injury claim can be handled while you stay home:

- **Meetings** by phone or video.
- **Documents** signed electronically.
- **Medical records** gathered from your Canadian doctors with your authorization.
- **Insurance negotiations** handled by your Washington lawyer.

If a case goes to litigation, there may be times you need to appear, such as a deposition or trial. Your lawyer will explain if and when that comes up, and some steps can be done remotely.

## The Washington Deadline

Washington's general statute of limitations for personal injury is three years (RCW 4.16.080). Some claims have shorter or special timelines, including claims against government entities, which can require advance notice. Evidence also fades: video footage gets recorded over and witnesses move. It is better to talk with a Washington lawyer early than to wait until a deadline is close.

## FAQ

### Can a Canadian sue for a car accident in Washington State?
Answer: Yes. You do not need to be a US resident to bring an injury claim in Washington when the crash happened there. Washington law generally governs the claim, and much of the case can be handled remotely from Canada.

### How long do I have to file an injury claim in Washington State?
Answer: Washington's general deadline for personal injury lawsuits is three years under RCW 4.16.080. Some claims, including those against government entities, have special notice rules or shorter timelines. Talking with a Washington lawyer early protects your options.

### Do I need a police report for a crash in Washington State?
Answer: Washington law (RCW 46.52.030) requires a driver involved in a collision that injures or kills someone to file a written report within four days, unless a law enforcement officer files one. If police came to the scene, get the agency name and report number. If no report was taken, ask the local police agency or a Washington lawyer what to do.

### Does ICBC cover me if I was injured in a crash in the United States?
Answer: ICBC states that all British Columbians injured in a crash in Canada or the United States have access to Enhanced Accident Benefits, regardless of fault. Report the crash to ICBC promptly. How those benefits interact with a claim against a Washington driver depends on your situation, so check the BC side with ICBC or a BC lawyer.

### Will my provincial health plan pay my US hospital bills?
Answer: Provincial plans may cover only part of emergency care outside Canada. Travel medical insurance often fills the gap, so call your insurer as soon as you can and keep every bill and receipt. Those costs may also be part of a Washington injury claim.

### Do I have to travel to Washington to handle my injury case?
Answer: Usually not for most of it. Meetings, signatures and records can be handled remotely, and you can keep treating with your own doctors at home. If the case goes to litigation, some steps may require an appearance, and your lawyer will explain the options.
