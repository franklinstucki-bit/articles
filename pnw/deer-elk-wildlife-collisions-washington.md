**Quick answer:** Hitting a deer or elk is usually handled through your own auto coverage, because an animal is not an at-fault driver. When another driver's conduct turned a wildlife encounter into a crash, such as tailgating or speeding, that driver may share responsibility.

It happens in a heartbeat. Headlights catch two green eyes on a dark road outside Spokane. Or an elk steps out of the timber on I-90 near Cle Elum, taller than your hood.

You brake. You swerve. Sometimes you hit the animal. Sometimes you miss it and hit something else.

## Where are wildlife collisions most common in Washington?

[WSDOT reports](https://wsdot.wa.gov/construction-planning/protecting-environment/reducing-risk-wildlife-collisions) that most reported wildlife-vehicle collisions involve deer and elk. Hotspots it names include areas near Spokane, the Methow and Okanogan valleys, Wenatchee, parts of I-90 near Easton and Cle Elum, Whidbey Island, and highways near Willapa Bay.

WSDOT also notes that rural highways with moderate traffic tend to see the highest collision rates. That describes a lot of the roads Washingtonians drive to cabins, trailheads and ferry docks.

The [I-90 Snoqualmie Pass East Project](https://wsdot.wa.gov/construction-planning/major-projects/i-90-snoqualmie-pass-east-project) has built a wildlife overcrossing, and its bridges and culverts are meant to reconnect habitat on both sides of the highway.

## Who pays when I hit a deer?

If no other driver was involved, there is usually no one to make a liability claim against. Your own policy is where to look:

- **PIP** may help with medical bills and lost wages, if you have it. See our PIP article.
- **Comprehensive or collision** may cover vehicle damage, depending on your policy and how the crash happened.

Read your declarations page. Coverage labels and deductibles vary.

## When can another driver be responsible?

Wildlife crashes often involve more than one vehicle. Examples:

- You stop for a deer on SR-20 and the driver behind you rear-ends you
- A driver ahead hits an elk, stops in the lane, and you cannot avoid them
- A speeding driver swerves around an animal and into your lane

Washington law expects drivers to adjust for hazards. [RCW 46.61.400](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=46.61.400) calls for reduced speed on narrow or winding roads and when special hazards exist. [RCW 46.61.145](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=46.61.145) prohibits following too closely.

Under Washington's [comparative fault rule](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=4.22.005), fault can be divided among drivers who each played a part.

## Should I swerve to avoid a deer?

That is a safety question more than a legal one, and it depends on the moment. Many drivers are taught to brake firmly and stay in their lane rather than swerve into oncoming traffic or off the road.

If you swerved and crashed, insurers may scrutinize your speed and reaction. Photos of the scene and any animal evidence help explain what happened.

## What should I do after a wildlife crash?

1. Move to safety and turn on your hazard lights.
2. Stay away from an injured animal. Deer and elk can still kick.
3. Call 911 if anyone is hurt or the animal blocks the road.
4. Photograph the vehicle, the road, and the animal or its tracks.
5. Report the crash to your insurer promptly.

WSDOT says dead animals on a state highway can be reported to the local State Patrol district or local law enforcement. It also notes that salvage of freshly killed deer and elk has been legal in most areas since 2016, with an online report. Check current rules before salvaging.

## Local specifics

On the San Juan Islands, black-tailed deer are part of daily life, and island roads are narrow and dark. Around Bellingham and the Chuckanut curves, deer cross at dusk. East of the Cascades, elk herds winter near the highways. Fall and early winter, when daylight shrinks, can mean more driving in the hours animals move.

## FAQ

### Is a deer collision considered my fault?

Not necessarily. It often goes through comprehensive coverage, and how your insurer treats it depends on your policy.

### What if I hit a car while avoiding a deer?

Fault depends on what each driver did. Speed, distance and lane position all matter.

### Do I need a police report for a deer crash?

If the crash caused injury, or damage above the State Patrol threshold, Washington's [reporting statute](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=46.52.030) may apply when no officer files a report.
