**Quick answer:** In Washington, most lawsuits for injuries from a car or motorcycle crash must be filed within three years under RCW 4.16.080(2). Some situations change the clock, including injured children and claims against a city, county or the state.

Three years sounds like a long time. In the Northwest it can pass in a few gray winters.

Treatment runs long. An insurer asks for one more record, then another. A case that seemed to be moving toward settlement stalls. Meanwhile the deadline does not stall with it.

## What is the general deadline?

[RCW 4.16.080](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=4.16.080) lists actions that "shall be commenced within three years." Subsection (2) includes actions for injury to the person or rights of another. That covers most crash injury lawsuits in Washington.

The deadline is for filing a lawsuit. Talking with an adjuster, opening a claim, or getting a settlement offer does not stop the clock.

## When does the clock start?

For most crashes, the clock is generally measured from the date of the collision. Some cases raise harder questions about when a claim accrued. Those are fact-specific and worth reviewing with a lawyer early rather than late.

## What about injured children?

[RCW 4.16.190](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=4.16.190) pauses the limitation period while the injured person is under eighteen, among other listed disabilities. The time of that disability is not counted.

That does not mean a family should wait. Evidence fades. Witnesses move. Medical bills incurred by parents can raise separate timing questions.

## What if a government vehicle or road was involved?

Claims against a city, county, transit agency or other local government have a pre-suit step. Under [RCW 4.96.020](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=4.96.020), a claim must first be presented to the entity's designated agent, and a lawsuit generally cannot be filed until sixty calendar days after the claim is presented. The limitation period is tolled during that waiting period.

Claims against the State of Washington follow a similar path under [RCW 4.92.110](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=4.92.110), with claims presented through the Office of Risk Management in the Department of Enterprise Services.

This can come up with a county vehicle on a San Juan Island road, a transit bus in Seattle, or a road-condition question on a state highway. The forms and content requirements matter.

## Are insurance claims on my own policy on the same clock?

Not always. A claim under your own policy, such as an underinsured motorist claim, may be governed by the policy language and different rules. Read your policy and ask early, because policies can contain their own notice and cooperation requirements.

## What should I do now, in practical terms?

1. Write down the crash date somewhere you will see it.
2. Gather the police report number and every insurance claim number.
3. Note whether any public vehicle, public employee or public roadway may be part of the story.
4. Keep your medical visits and records organized by date.
5. Talk with a lawyer well before the deadline, not in the final weeks.

Lawyers need time to investigate, request records, and file properly. A rushed filing is a harder filing.

## Local specifics

The Northwest's geography stretches timelines. Clients in Friday Harbor travel by ferry to see specialists. People hurt on Stevens Pass may live in Wenatchee but treat in Everett. Visitors hurt in Seattle may fly home to another state before the first bill arrives.

None of that pauses the deadline. It just makes an early start more valuable.

## FAQ

### Is the deadline different for motorcycle crashes?

The general three-year rule in RCW 4.16.080(2) applies to injury claims whether you were in a car or on a motorcycle. Special circumstances can change that.

### Does a settlement offer extend the deadline?

No. Negotiations do not stop the clock. Only certain legal events do.

### What happens if I miss it?

A late lawsuit can be dismissed. That is why the date matters more than any other single detail early in a case.
