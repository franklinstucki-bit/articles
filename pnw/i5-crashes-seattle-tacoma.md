**Quick answer:** Crashes on I-5 between Everett, Seattle and Tacoma are often rear-end and lane-change collisions in stop-and-go traffic, made worse by rain and spray. Washington's rules on safe speed and following distance, plus early evidence, usually shape how fault is decided.

I-5 is the spine of western Washington. It carries commuters, freight, ferry traffic heading for Edmonds and Mukilteo, and travelers bound for Portland or Vancouver, B.C.

It also carries risk. Lanes appear and vanish. Exits come up on the left. Spray from a semi turns a windshield white in a second.

## Why are I-5 crashes so common in Seattle and Tacoma?

The corridor combines heavy volume with tight geometry. Familiar trouble spots include:

- The Ship Canal Bridge and the express-lane transitions north of downtown Seattle
- The downtown Seattle stretch near the convention center, with closely spaced exits and merges
- The I-5 and I-405 interchanges at Tukwila and Lynnwood
- The curves near the Tacoma Dome and the traffic through Fife
- The Joint Base Lewis-McChord corridor, where speeds shift suddenly toward Olympia

Add a November rain, low afternoon light, and a driver glancing at a phone. That is how most of these collisions begin.

## Who is at fault in a rear-end crash on I-5?

Often, but not always, the following driver. Under [RCW 46.61.145](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=46.61.145), a driver must not follow more closely than is "reasonable and prudent," considering speed, traffic and road conditions.

[RCW 46.61.400](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=46.61.400) adds that speed must be reasonable and prudent under the conditions. On wet pavement, the speed limit is not a safe harbor.

Insurers sometimes argue the lead driver stopped suddenly or cut in. Under Washington's [pure comparative fault rule](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=4.22.005), that kind of argument can reduce a recovery without barring it.

## What about lane-change and merge crashes?

These are harder. Two drivers often tell two different stories. Evidence that helps includes:

- Dash-cam video, yours or another driver's
- Witness names and phone numbers, collected at the scene
- Photos of where each car came to rest, before they are moved if it is safe
- The location of damage on each vehicle, which can show angle and movement

WSDOT traffic cameras exist along much of the corridor, but footage may not be kept. Ask early if you think a camera caught it.

## Should I move my car after an I-5 crash?

For a damage-only collision, [RCW 46.52.020(2)](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=46.52.020) directs drivers to move vehicles as soon as possible to a safe spot like the shoulder of an exit ramp or a nearby cross street. Moving the vehicle does not affect fault.

If anyone is injured, stop at or as close to the scene as possible, call 911, and give reasonable assistance. Stay out of live lanes.

## Do I need to file a report?

Often an officer will handle the report. When no officer makes one, [RCW 46.52.030](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=46.52.030) requires drivers to file a written report within four days if the crash caused injury, death, or property damage above the threshold set by the State Patrol.

## What should I do in the days after?

1. Get checked by a doctor, even if you feel only stiff. Whiplash and concussion symptoms can appear later.
2. Open a claim with your own insurer and ask about PIP.
3. Request the police report by number.
4. Save your dash-cam footage before it records over.
5. Talk to a lawyer before giving the other driver's insurer a recorded statement; your own insurer may require cooperation under your policy.

## FAQ

### What if a semi-truck caused the crash?

Truck crashes can involve additional parties and federal safety rules. Preserving evidence quickly matters even more.

### What if multiple cars were involved?

Chain-reaction crashes on I-5 can split fault among several drivers. Washington's comparative fault rule allows that division.

### Can I get the WSDOT camera footage?

Sometimes. Footage may not be stored, so it is worth asking right away.
