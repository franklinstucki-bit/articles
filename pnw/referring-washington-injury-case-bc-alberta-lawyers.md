## Why a Washington State Crash Claim Usually Belongs in Washington

Injury claims tend to follow the place of the collision. The at-fault driver, the witnesses, the investigating officer, the scene and the insurance policy are often all in Washington. Washington courts have clear authority over a driver who caused a crash on a Washington road, and Washington's statutes and procedural rules will usually govern the claim.

That does not mean your client has to move to Seattle or Bellingham. Most of the work in a Washington injury claim, from gathering records to negotiating with the insurer, can be done remotely. Your client can keep treating with their own doctors at home.

The same logic runs the other way. If a Washington resident was hurt in a crash in BC, that case is likely a BC matter, and Frank will send it back across the border to Canadian counsel. That is the two-way part of the desk.

## Key Washington Rules for Canadian Lawyers

### The Washington three-year deadline (RCW 4.16.080)

Washington's general statute of limitations for personal injury is three years. RCW 4.16.080 lists actions that "shall be commenced within three years," including actions "for any other injury to the person or rights of another." There are exceptions and special rules, and claims against government entities can carry their own notice requirements, so do not treat three years as a safe cushion. The earlier a file reaches Washington counsel, the more options the client has.

### Pure comparative fault (RCW 4.22.005)

Washington follows pure comparative fault. Under RCW 4.22.005, a claimant's own fault "diminishes proportionately" the damages awarded "but does not bar recovery." In plain terms, if a jury found your client 30 percent at fault, the award is reduced by 30 percent rather than wiped out. This matters for messy liability files, such as multi-vehicle collisions on I-5 or winter crashes on the way to Mt. Baker, that a client might assume are not worth pursuing.

### Contingency fees are common in Washington

Personal injury work in Washington is commonly handled on a contingency fee, meaning the lawyer is paid a percentage of any recovery and nothing if there is no recovery. Fee terms are put in a written agreement with the client. Your client can ask Frank directly about his terms before signing anything.

## How ICBC Enhanced Care Affects BC Residents Injured in the US

Since May 1, 2021, BC auto insurance has run on ICBC's Enhanced Care model, a care-based (no-fault) system. ICBC states that all British Columbians injured in a crash in Canada or the United States have access to Enhanced Accident Benefits, regardless of who caused the crash.

So a BC resident hurt in Washington will often have two tracks to think about:

- **ICBC benefits** through Enhanced Care, which the client should report and apply for promptly.
- **A potential Washington claim** against the at-fault driver and their insurer, governed by Washington law.

How those two tracks interact, including any coordination, repayment or deduction issues on the ICBC side, depends on the facts and on BC law. That is a question for the BC lawyer, and for ICBC, to work through alongside Frank. Frank handles the Washington claim; he does not give advice on BC law or ICBC entitlements.

Alberta drivers are in transition. Alberta has announced its Care-First system, beginning January 1, 2027. If your Alberta client's crash falls near that change, check how the new rules apply with Alberta counsel or the client's insurer.

## What Frank Needs to Evaluate a Washington Injury File

When your client has agreed to be referred, these items let Frank give a fast, honest read on the Washington claim:

1. **The police or collision report**, or at least the agency name and report number (Washington State Patrol, Bellingham Police, Seattle Police, a county sheriff).
2. **Insurance information** for every vehicle involved, including the client's own policy.
3. **Photos** of the vehicles, the scene and visible injuries.
4. **A short medical summary**: where the client was treated, and current treatment status. Full records can follow later.

Gather these with your client's consent. If you only have part of the list, send what you have. Missing pieces can usually be obtained in Washington.

## FAQ

### Can a Canadian lawyer refer a Washington State car accident case to a US lawyer?
Answer: Yes. A Canadian lawyer can introduce a client to a Washington-licensed lawyer, with the client's consent, when the crash happened in Washington. The Washington lawyer handles the Washington claim, and the Canadian lawyer can continue to handle any BC or Alberta matters, such as ICBC benefits.

### How long does a Canadian have to file an injury lawsuit in Washington State?
Answer: Washington's general deadline for personal injury lawsuits is three years under RCW 4.16.080. Exceptions and special notice rules can apply, especially for claims involving government entities. Because deadlines can be shorter than they look, it is best to have a Washington lawyer review the file early.

### Does ICBC cover BC residents injured in a crash in Washington State?
Answer: ICBC states that all British Columbians injured in a crash in Canada or the United States have access to Enhanced Accident Benefits, regardless of fault. How those benefits interact with a Washington claim against the at-fault driver depends on the facts and BC law, so BC counsel or ICBC should confirm the BC side.

### What if my client was partly at fault for the Washington crash?
Answer: Washington uses pure comparative fault under RCW 4.22.005. The client's share of fault reduces the damages proportionately but does not bar recovery. A client found partly responsible may still have a claim worth reviewing.

### What documents should I send to evaluate a Washington injury case?
Answer: The most useful items are the police or collision report, insurance information for all vehicles, photos, and a short medical summary. Gather them with the client's consent. Partial information is fine to start.
